## Summary

4on4 is QuakeWorld's flagship competitive team format: two teams of four fight to out-frag each other over a 20-minute match. Its defining trait is that picked-up weapons don't stay on the ground, which turns the map's weapons, armor, and powerups into contested resources -- so 4on4 is as much about controlling the map as about aim. It's the mode the organized QuakeWorld scene is built around, played on a small set of classic maps and run through long-standing leagues. You start a game with `/4on4`.

## Activate

On a KTX server, type `/4on4` in the console. Any player can do it, or an admin spectator. The server applies the 4on4 preset and teams ready up. You can append a match tag for QTV event tooling: `4on4 EQL`.

`/4on4` is refused if the server doesn't allow the mode (see *Hosting & settings*), on hoonymode-only maps, and while the auto-XonX feature is active.

## Basic ruleset

Activating 4on4 applies a fixed competitive preset -- an admin doesn't normally tune it per match:

- **`deathmatch 1`** -- weapons don't stay on the floor after pickup. The single most defining setting, and the reason 4on4 is a map-control game (see *How it plays*).
- **`maxclients 8` / `teamplay 2`** -- eight players, two teams, friendly fire on.
- **`timelimit 20`** with **`k_overtime 1` / `k_exttime 5`** -- 20-minute matches; a draw goes to timed overtime in 5-minute periods (more than one if the tie holds).
- **`k_pow 1`** -- powerups spawn on the map (`dq` / `dr` left off, so they aren't dropped on death).
- **`k_membercount 3`** -- each team needs three ready players before the match can begin.

## How it plays

Two teams of four, friendly fire on. You win by holding the higher frag count when the clock runs out; a drawn match goes to overtime -- a 5-minute period by default, and it can run more than once if the tie holds.

The foundation is the weapon economy. When a weapon is picked up it doesn't stay on the floor -- it respawns about 30 seconds later -- so the map's contents become finite, timed resources. But weapons are the least of it: the resources that actually decide a 4on4 are the **armors** (the red armor above all) and the **powerups**.

So 4on4 is a timing-and-control game, not a fragfest. The skill is item timing -- the red armor cycles every 20 seconds, the Quad 60 seconds after it's last taken -- and firepower distribution: making sure the player holding the rocket launcher also holds the armor, passing items to whoever can use them, and reading the map so your team owns the next armor and the next Quad before the enemy does. A Quad timed onto an armored push is what breaks a game open; the weapons are just the means to win the fights that let you hold those resources. It's as much a communication game as a shooting one -- teams run on constant reporting (enemy positions, your own health and weapon, who needs what, when the next powerup is up) through QuakeWorld's teamsay messages.

Powerups are live -- Quad Damage, Pentagram, and Ring of Shadows -- but they aren't dropped when their carrier dies. A Quad is a 60-second window the holding team presses while it lasts, not an item that changes hands on a kill.

## Maps

4on4 is played on a small, stable pool, not the full id1 rotation -- scene conventions, not anything KTX enforces (any map loads, but organized play sticks to the staples, gathered on the community's `maphub_v2` hub).

- **TB3 -- "the big three":** `dm3` (the definitive 4on4 map), `dm2`, `e1m2`. The canonical core, played for decades.
- **The staples beyond TB3:** `schloss` has slowly earned the fourth spot -- the long-running **thebig4.se** tournament is named for it -- and a rotating fifth slot rounds out a five-map pool (recently `phantombase`).
- **"Kenya":** the scene's half-derisive term for the wider pool of lesser-played custom maps -- anything that isn't the sacred TB3 (the `cmt` series and the like). The label caught on as grumbling about being pulled off the big three; there's even a joke `kenya.bsp`.

## History

4on4 predates KTX -- it was the staple team format on KTPro servers before KTX became the standard QuakeWorld server mod, and KTX carried it forward as one of its built-in usermodes. It's the format the competitive scene has organized itself around for decades, through leagues such as NQR (Nations Quake Rumble) and EQL (European Quake League) and a long line of well-known clans.

## Hosting & settings

On a stock KTX or nquake server 4on4 is available by default -- the allow-list (`k_allowed_free_modes`) defaults to `4095`, every mode. For a 4on4-only server:

```
set k_defmode 4on4
set k_allowed_free_modes 8    // allow nothing else  (default 4095 = all modes)
```

Bit `8` is shared with `ca` and `wipeout`, so restricting to it allows those two as well. See *server-setup* for the bitmask details.

After applying the preset, 4on4 also execs any `configs/usermodes/4on4/*.cfg` on the server, so an operator can layer their own overrides. The "approved rules" leagues publish -- which sometimes add settings such as `fallbunny` -- are league and server conventions layered on top; they are not part of the KTX `/4on4` preset.
